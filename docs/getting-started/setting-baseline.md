# Setting Your Baseline

Once you have imported your everyday bank transactions into the `source` tab, you might notice that your Net Worth on the Dashboard still feels incomplete. 

To get a truly comprehensive view of your financial health, you need to set up your long-term wealth trackers: **Investments**, **Capital**, and **Debt**, as well as define your **Yearly Budgets**.

## Tracking Long-Term Wealth

These specialized tabs are crucial for tracking financial components that don't fluctuate with daily transactions but have a massive impact on your net worth.

### 📈 Investments Tab
If you hold stocks, ETFs, crypto, or other investments, record them here. 
- **Broker**: Where the asset is held (e.g. Vanguard, Coinbase).
- **Item / Ticker**: The name or symbol of the investment.
- **Amount & Value**: How many units you own and their current value.

### 🏠 Capital Tab
Use this tab to track valuable illiquid assets.
- **Item & Value**: Enter things like the estimated value of your house, a car, expensive electronics, or fine jewelry.

### 😓 Debt Tab
Enter your liabilities here, such as a mortgage or student loans.
- **Amount & Left to pay**: The total initial debt versus what remains.
- **Interest & Period**: The rate you are paying and the timeline for paying it off. 

> [!NOTE]
> Values entered across these three tabs are automatically aggregated and fed into the right-hand column of your `dashboard` to calculate your true **Net Worth**.

## Creating Yearly Budgets

Budgeting in YMYL is handled via dedicated year tabs (e.g., `2026`, `2027`).

1. Open the tab for the current year.
2. The tab features a matrix of your customized Categories (rows) against the 12 months of the year (columns).
3. Enter your expected income (positive numbers) and expected expenses (negative numbers) for each category across the months.

As you import transactions throughout the year, the dashboard and background calculations will automatically compare your actual spending against the budget you laid out here, giving you deep insights into your spending habits.
